Questions, answered

What permissions does macOS ask for, and why?

Three: Microphone (to record your voice), Accessibility (to paste text into the focused app), and Input Monitoring (to detect the Right Option hotkey globally). Voza explains each one on first launch. Nothing is sent anywhere — these are local capabilities macOS gates for your protection.

How do I get the speech model?

You don't have to. Voza detects your hardware and downloads the right one (with SHA-256 verification) from the official whisper.cpp releases the first time you open Settings → Models. It's a one-time 190 MB download for most Macs.

Does the AI cleanup need an API key?

No. If you have Ollama installed, cleanup runs on your Mac for free (qwen3:8b by default). If you don't, you can drop a key into ~/.config/voza/.env — or leave cleanup off and get plain transcriptions.

Why Right Option instead of a keyboard shortcut?

Right Option is almost never used by macOS or other apps, sits right under your thumb's resting arc, and is semantically perfect for "hold to talk" — the same muscle memory as a walkie-talkie. Both hotkeys are remappable in config.

What happens to my audio?

It's written to a temporary WAV, transcribed, and deleted immediately — in the same breath. The transcript goes to your clipboard and your local ~/Dictation log. That's the entire lifecycle.

Is this open source?

Yes — MIT licensed. Audit every line, build it yourself with ./build.sh, or buy the pre-built, signed copy to skip the hassle. Same code, your choice.
